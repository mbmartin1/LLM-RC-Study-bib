**Why it is here:** dataset candidate #3 — roughly 25,000 argumentative essays from 15 prompts, written by 6th–12th graders on a state assessment.

**In favor:** the prompt structure is explicit and small in number, which makes generating matched model responses straightforward. General-interest argumentative topics avoid the domain-knowledge confound that rules against [[mohler-2011-short-answer-grading]]. It is documented and citable, unlike the ASAP access situation.

**Against (from the proposal):** writing quality varies a lot across school grades, so reading time may be driven by disfluent student writing rather than by the efficiency properties of interest. The human reference condition would then be a moving target.

**Thought worth developing:** that variance may be an asset rather than a liability. If human writing spans a wide quality range, we can ask where model outputs fall *within* the human distribution rather than treating humans as one point — which is a stronger test than a two-group comparison.

**To check:** licensing and redistribution terms for using passages as stimuli in an online reading study.
