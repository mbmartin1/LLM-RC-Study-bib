**Why it is here:** the source for uniform information density (UID), the second efficiency signature the study tests. Speakers modulate optional reduction so that information is spread more evenly across the utterance.

**How it is used:** UID gives a prediction with a clear shape — it is about the *variance* of surprisal across a passage, not its mean. That matters for the design: a model could match human mean surprisal while being much spikier, and UID says the spiky text should cost readers more. This is probably the sharpest link between the descriptive stats and the reading-time predictions.

**Dependency worth flagging:** operationalizing UID requires a surprisal estimator, which drags in the estimator question raised by [[oh-2023-surprisal-larger-models]]. Whatever estimator we pick has to be defensible for both the UID measure and the reading-time analysis.
